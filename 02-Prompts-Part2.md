## Category 3: Email Marketing (15 Prompts) — Continued

### Newsletter Templates

**Prompt 41:**
```
Write a monthly email newsletter for a [type of business]. Include: 5 subject lines under 50 characters (intriguing, not clickbait), a warm opening (50-75 words), 3 short updates (customer story, behind-the-scenes, useful tip), and one clear call to action. Keep total under 500 words. Write like a real human.
```

**Prompt 42:**
```
Write a weekly email for a [type of business] that includes: a personal opener (2-3 sentences), one main update or story (100-150 words), a quick tip or insight, and a soft CTA. Keep it short enough to read in 2 minutes. Under 300 words total.
```

**Prompt 43:**
```
You own a [type of business]. Write an email to reconnect with customers who haven't visited or purchased in the last 3 months. Include: a warm, non-guilt-inducing opener, a genuine reason to come back (new offering, reminder of what they loved), and an easy next step. Under 200 words.
```

### Promotional Emails

**Prompt 44:**
```
Write a promotional email for a [type of business] announcing [your offer]. Include: 3 subject lines (under 50 chars each), preview text (under 100 chars), opening paragraph that grabs attention, the offer details (what, why, when), and a clear CTA. Create urgency without being pushy. Under 250 words total.
```

**Prompt 45:**
```
You run a [type of business]. Write an email announcing a seasonal promotion for [holiday/season/event]. Make it feel timely and relevant, not just a "buy now" message. Include: what's being offered, why now matters, how to claim it, and a deadline that creates gentle urgency. Under 200 words.
```

**Prompt 46:**
```
Write a "We miss you" email for a [type of business] targeting customers who haven't visited in [time period]. Tone should be warm and understanding, not guilt-tripping. Include: genuine miss you message, something new you've added, an incentive to come back, and easy booking/ordering info. Under 180 words.
```

### Welcome Sequences

**Prompt 47:**
```
Create a 3-email welcome sequence for new subscribers to a [type of business]. 
Email 1 (Day 1): Welcome, introduce what makes you different, set expectations for what they'll receive
Email 2 (Day 3): Share your best stuff — a customer story, your best tip, or behind-the-scenes
Email 3 (Day 7): Make a soft ask — visit us, book something, or take a specific action
Each should be under 200 words, feel personal, and have a clear single goal.
```

**Prompt 48:**
```
You just got a new email subscriber to your [type of business]. Write a welcome email that: thanks them, tells them what to expect from you (email frequency, type of content), shares one useful thing right away, and invites them to reply if they have questions. Under 150 words.
```

### Other Email

**Prompt 49:**
```
Write an email for a [type of business] to send to someone who started a booking or order but didn't complete it. Include: friendly check-in (no pressure), what they left behind, a clear button to return and complete, and a small incentive if appropriate. Under 150 words. Make it feel helpful, not pushy.
```

**Prompt 50:**
```
You run a [type of business]. Write a "booking reminder" email for someone who has an upcoming appointment. Include: appointment details, what to bring or prepare, what happens if they need to reschedule, and a friendly reminder of why their appointment matters. Under 120 words.
```

## Category 4: Menu & Service Descriptions (15 Prompts)

**Prompt 56:**
```
You are a professional copywriter for restaurants. Rewrite these menu item descriptions to make them more appetizing and sales-focused. Keep descriptions 10-20 words. Use sensory words (smell, taste, texture). Make people want to order.

[LIST MENU ITEMS]
```

**Prompt 57:**
```
Write menu descriptions for a [type of cuisine] restaurant's signature dishes. For each item include: the dish name, a 15-25 word description that highlights key ingredients and flavors, and a suggested drink pairing. Descriptions should make you hungry just reading them.
```

**Prompt 58:**
```
Create a seasonal menu section for a [type of restaurant]. Write descriptions for [list seasonal items]. Each description should: highlight freshness and seasonality, use appetizing sensory language, be 15-25 words, and include one unique angle (farm source, chef's inspiration, local ingredient, etc.).
```

**Prompt 59:**
```
Write a cocktail/menu item description for a [type of bar/restaurant] that makes the drink sound irresistible. Include: the drink name, a 20-30 word description with sensory details (look, smell, taste, texture), and a suggested food pairing. Make it sound like something you'd order immediately.
```

**Prompt 60:**
```
You run a [type of restaurant]. Write descriptions for your "most popular" or signature dishes that go on a specials board or featured section of the menu. Each description: names the dish, describes it appetizingly in 1-2 sentences, and includes a price. Make them compelling enough to order right now.
```

**Prompt 61:**
```
You are a professional copywriter for [type of service business]. Rewrite these service descriptions to be more compelling and clear. Show the benefit, not just the feature. Keep each to 2-3 sentences. Make someone who's never been feel comfortable and excited to book.

[LIST SERVICES]
```

**Prompt 62:**
```
Write booking descriptions for a [type of salon/spa]. For each service include: service name, what happens (1-2 sentences), who it's for (ideal client), duration and price, what to expect before/during/after. Make descriptions sound welcoming to first-timers. Avoid jargon.

Services: [LIST]
```

**Prompt 63:**
```
Write benefit-focused descriptions for a [type of clinic/practice]'s main services. Each description should: start with the outcome the customer gets, explain briefly how it works and why it's effective, mention any special equipment or approach that makes you different, and include a price range if appropriate. 3-4 sentences each.
```

**Prompt 64:**
```
You run a [type of service business]. Write descriptions for your most popular service packages or bundles. Each should: name the package, list what's included, highlight the value (what they save vs. buying separately), and make it clear who it's best for. 3-5 sentences each.
```

**Prompt 65:**
```
Create a pricing page description for a [type of business] that explains your pricing clearly and positions it as valuable. Include: how your pricing works (per session, package, membership, etc.), what affects price, what's included, and why it's worth it. Address the question "why is this more/less expensive than competitors?" under 300 words.
```

**Prompt 66:**
```
Write a membership or subscription description for a [type of business]. Include: what members get, what it costs, the benefits of committing vs. pay-as-you-go, and a clear CTA to join. Make the annual option feel like a steal if both are available. Under 200 words.
```

**Prompt 67:**
```
You run a [type of business]. Write an "upsell" description for your premium or deluxe version of [service/product]. Highlight what's extra and why it's worth upgrading. Make it sound like a treat, not just more stuff. Under 100 words.
```

**Prompt 68:**
```
Write "transformation" descriptions for a [type of business] that show customers what they can expect. Each should include: the starting point or problem, what the process is, and the end result. Make it feel achievable and appealing. Use language that doesn't promise impossible results but does communicate real change.
```

**Prompt 69:**
```
You own a [type of business]. Write a comparison description for 2-3 of your most popular service tiers or product options. For each: name, price, what's included, and who it's best for. Make the middle option feel like the smart choice without making the others seem bad.
```

**Prompt 70:**
```
Write a "Why Choose Us" section for a [type of business]'s menu or service page. Cover: what makes your [restaurant/salon/clinic/business] different from others, your approach or philosophy, what customers can expect in quality and experience, and 2-3 specific things you do that competitors don't. Under 250 words.
```

```

**Prompt 72:**
```
Create a "what to expect" message for first-time customers of a [type of business]. Include: when to arrive, what will happen during the visit, what to bring, how long it takes, and how to reach you with questions. Warm and informative tone. Under 180 words.
```

**Prompt 73:**
```
Write a friendly appointment reminder for a [type of business] to send 24 hours before a scheduled visit. Include: appointment details, a friendly reminder, what to do if they need to reschedule, and a genuine offer to help with any questions. Under 100 words.
```

**Prompt 74:**
```
Create a same-day appointment reminder for a [type of business]. Include: their appointment is coming up today, when to arrive, what to expect, and a warm welcome message. Keep it short and reassuring. Under 80 words.
```

**Prompt 75:**
```
You run a [type of business]. Write a "we haven't seen you lately" reminder message for customers who had an ongoing treatment or series of appointments but haven't booked follow-ups. Include: warm check-in, what's been going on with your business, an invitation to book, and easy ways to reach you. Under 150 words.

**Prompt 76:**
```
Write a post-visit thank you message for a [type of business]. Include: thanks for visiting, what they should do next (follow-up appointment, aftercare, etc.), how to reach you with questions, and an invitation to share feedback or refer friends. Under 120 words.

**Prompt 77:**
```
Create a post-service follow-up for a [type of business] that includes: a genuine thank you, any aftercare instructions or next steps, a suggested timeline for when to return, and a soft ask to leave a review if they were happy. Under 150 words.
```

**Prompt 78:**
```
Write 10 text message templates for a [type of business] that customers might receive. Include variations for: appointment reminders, confirmations, follow-ups, promotions, and general communication. Each should: sound natural on text, be under 160 characters, and have a clear purpose.
```

**Prompt 79:**
```
You run a [type of business]. Write a "running late" notification to send to customers when you're behind schedule. Include: apologize sincerely, give updated wait time, offer to reschedule if the wait is too long, and thank them for their patience. Under 100 words.
```

**Prompt 80:**
```
Write a response template for a [type of business] to use when a customer asks about pricing over text or DM. Include: friendly acknowledgment, what information you need from them, how you'll get back to them, and an invitation to book a consultation if appropriate. Under 100 words.
```

**Prompt 81:**
```
Write a message for a [type of business] to send when there's been a scheduling error or your business needs to reschedule a customer's appointment. Include: apologize sincerely, explain what happened, offer options (new time, different provider, compensation), and express genuine regret for the inconvenience. Under 150 words.
```

**Prompt 82:**
```
You run a [type of business]. Write a response to use when a customer is unhappy with their service or product. Include: genuine empathy with their frustration, acknowledgment of their experience, what you're doing to make it right, and an invitation to discuss further. Under 180 words.
```

**Prompt 83:**
```
Write a "cancellation policy" message for a [type of business] that explains your policy clearly and kindly. Include: what the policy is, why it exists (protects both sides), how to avoid fees, and an invitation to reach out with questions. Make it firm but friendly. Under 120 words.
```

**Prompt 84:**
```
You manage a [type of business]. Write a message to inform a customer that they're on a waitlist for a booking slot they wanted. Include: thanks for their patience, their position on the list, when they can expect to hear back, and what to do if they find another option in the meantime. Under 100 words.
```

**Prompt 85:**
```
Write a "multi-location" message for a [type of business] with multiple locations to help customers find the right one. Include: a warm greeting, questions to help them choose the right location, directions to each location, and easy ways to contact you for help. Under 150 words.

## Category 6: Website & Landing Pages (15 Prompts)

**Prompt 86:**
```
You own a [type of business]. Write a homepage hero section that includes: headline (8-10 words, benefit-focused), subheadline (1-2 sentences expanding on the headline), and a primary CTA button (3-5 words). Make the headline benefit-driven, not feature-driven. Speak to the customer, not yourself.
```

**Prompt 87:**
```
Write a homepage intro paragraph for a [type of business]. Include: what you do, who you serve, what makes you different, and why customers should choose you over competitors. Tone should be confident but not arrogant, warm but not fluffy. Under 200 words.
```

**Prompt 88:**
```
Write an "About Us" page for a [type of business]. Include: how you started and why, what you believe about serving customers, what makes your team or approach different, and what you want customers to feel when they work with you. Under 400 words. Make it personal, not corporate.
```

**Prompt 89:**
```
You own a [type of business]. Write a team or staff introduction section for your website. For [3 team members]: include their name, role, experience or background, what they love about their work, and one personal detail that makes them relatable. Each 50-75 words.
```

**Prompt 90:**
```
Write a services overview for a [type of business] that includes: an opening paragraph explaining your approach to your work, descriptions of your 3-5 main services (2-3 sentences each), and a closing paragraph about what customers can expect when working with you. Under 400 words total.
```

**Prompt 91:**
```
You run a [type of business]. Write a "What to Expect" page that walks customers through the entire process of working with you — from first contact to after-service follow-up. Include: how to get started, what happens during the service/experience, what happens after, and what makes your process different from others. Under 350 words.
```

**Prompt 92:**
```
Write a contact page for a [type of business] that includes: a welcoming intro, multiple ways to reach you (phone, email, form, location), hours of operation, and a map placeholder. Make it easy for someone who's never been to find and contact you. Under 200 words.
```

**Prompt 93:**
```
Create a CTA (call-to-action) page or section for a [type of business]. Include: a compelling headline (under 10 words), a brief explanation of what they're getting when they take action, a primary button (3-5 words), and a secondary option. Make it feel low-pressure and clear.
```

**Prompt 94:**
```
Create a FAQ page for a [type of business]. Think from a customer perspective: what would they want to know before booking, ordering, or visiting? Cover: pricing and payment, booking and appointments, first-timer information, common concerns, and anything unique to your business. Question → answer format, under 800 words total.
```

**Prompt 95:**
```
You own a [type of business]. Write a "Do you offer..." type FAQ for your most common questions. Include 10 questions customers frequently ask you, with complete but concise answers. Make it scannable and genuinely helpful, not just checking boxes.
```

**Prompt 96:**
```
Write a "Why Choose Us" page for a [type of business] that explains what makes you different. Include: your approach or philosophy, 3 specific things you do that competitors don't, your credentials or experience, and what customers have said about working with you (testimonial placeholder section). Under 350 words.
```

**Prompt 97:**
```
Create a testimonial highlight section for a [type of business]'s website. Include: 5 customer quotes (placeholder with real customer language), attributed to [customer type, city], and organized to address different concerns (quality, reliability, value, experience, results).
```

**Prompt 98:**
```
Write a "Serving [City/Area]" page for a [type of business] that ranks well for local search. Include: what areas you serve and why you chose them, neighborhood or area-specific details that show you know the community, landmark references that help with directions, and a map section placeholder. Under 300 words.
```

**Prompt 99:**
```
You own a [type of business]. Write a local SEO-optimized description for your Google Business Profile that includes: what you do, who it's for, what makes you different, and your main service areas. Under 350 characters. Include primary keywords naturally.
```

**Prompt 100:**
```
Create a "New to [City/Area]? Here's Where to Find Us" landing page for a [type of business]. Include: directions from major landmarks or highways, parking information, what to look for when approaching, and a warm invitation to visit. Make it feel like you're welcoming them into your space before they arrive. Under 250 words.
```
